People buy from people. I say that a lot, because it gets to the heart of something that can get lost when we're talking about photos, reels, and how often to post.

Someone considering you as their real estate agent wants to know what it will be like to work with you. Will you explain things clearly? Will you understand what matters to them? Will they feel comfortable picking up the phone when something gets stressful?

Your marketing can start answering those questions before you've met.

That's what I mean by personal branding. It doesn't require turning your life into content or becoming the loudest person on Instagram. It means making your experience, personality, and approach easier for a prospective client to recognize.

## A beautiful listing tells only part of your story

Professional real estate photography helps someone see the property you're representing. A well-planned video can help them picture the experience of being there. Those are important jobs.

But a person looking through your website may be deciding whether to hire you to sell a completely different home. They also need to understand what you bring to that relationship.

This is where I like to see agents put some of themselves into their marketing. Explain why you chose a particular approach for a property. Talk about a question a seller should ask before listing. Share how you help someone prepare for photography without making the whole process overwhelming.

Now the viewer gets to see your judgment, not just the finished result.

You don't have to give away private client information to do this. You can explain a general situation, show an approved example, or answer the question without identifying the person who asked it.

## What should an agent actually talk about on camera?

Start with something you've explained this week.

Maybe a seller asked whether every room needed to be photographed. Maybe a buyer wanted to understand what to look for during a second showing. Maybe someone was confused about what happens between accepting an offer and getting to the closing table.

Choose a question within your expertise and answer it as you would across a table. Keep it focused. Give the viewer something useful before asking them to contact you.

For example, a video could begin: "Before we schedule your listing photos, here are two things I want you to think about." Then explain those two things. No elaborate introduction is necessary.

Your personality comes through in the way you answer. A calm, thoughtful agent doesn't need a high-energy performance to make a good video. A naturally animated agent doesn't need to flatten their personality to sound professional.

## You can be recognizable without sharing everything

There's a difference between being approachable and making your private life public.

Some agents are comfortable sharing family moments and daily routines. Others would rather keep the focus on their work. Both can create a personal brand.

You can show your face, explain your process, talk about what you enjoy about a neighborhood, and share your perspective without discussing anything you want to keep private. You can also use an interview format, where someone asks the questions and you concentrate on answering them.

I would rather help you find a format you're comfortable repeating than push you into one impressive video you'll never want to make again.

## Give people a few ways to get to know you

A useful starting point is an introduction, an educational video, and a client story.

The introduction explains who you help and how you approach your work. The educational video shows that you can be helpful. The client story, shared with permission, gives someone else's perspective on the experience.

These can live on your website as well as your social channels. Someone arriving through a referral should be able to find the same person their friend described, rather than a collection of generic marketing lines.

For agents working across Long Island and the Hamptons, that also means being specific about the places and types of properties you actually know. You don't need to claim expertise everywhere. Give people a clear sense of where you're useful.

Before planning your next video, think about the question a potential client would ask before trusting you with their home. Start there. A useful, honest answer will sound far more like you than another paragraph about being passionate, dedicated, and results-driven.

Give people something real to connect with. Then make it easy for them to start a conversation.
