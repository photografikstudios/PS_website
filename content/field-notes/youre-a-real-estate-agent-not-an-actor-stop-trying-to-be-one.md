You can walk a client through a property, answer an unexpected question, and explain a complicated situation without much trouble. Then a camera turns on and suddenly you can't remember how you normally talk.

That doesn't mean you're bad at video. It may mean we're asking you to work in a way that doesn't suit you.

I've talked about this with our mobile teleprompter setup because the goal is simple: make life easier for the agent. You're there to explain something you know, not spend the afternoon memorizing lines.

You don't need to become an actor. We need to build a process that lets you be yourself with a camera in the room.

## Start with words you would actually say

A script can be grammatically perfect and still be impossible to read naturally.

Long sentences, unfamiliar phrases, and formal introductions can make someone sound very different from the person their clients know. If you wouldn't say "an unparalleled living experience" during a showing, there's no reason to force it into your video.

Start by explaining the point out loud. Then build the script around the language you actually use.

For a listing video, that might mean one clear observation about the home rather than a paragraph of adjectives. For an educational video, it might mean answering one client question and giving an example.

When I review a script, I want to hear a person talking. If it reads like brochure copy, we have more work to do before recording.

## A teleprompter is support, not a personality replacement

A teleprompter places your words where you can read them close to the camera lens. Used thoughtfully, it lets you concentrate on delivery instead of trying to remember the next sentence.

But putting a stiff script on a screen doesn't magically make it conversational.

The text needs to fit you. The pace needs to leave room for breathing and emphasis. And sometimes the best thing to do is stop reading, explain the thought in your own words, and adjust the script around that.

For short sections, a few prompts may be enough. For exact wording, a fuller script may be useful. We can choose the level of support based on the person and the job, rather than make everyone use the same method.

## You don't have to get everything right in one take

There's no prize for remembering a whole property presentation in one attempt.

We can record a thought, pause, and move to the next one. Property footage, detail shots, and other relevant visuals can give us natural places to edit, as long as the final result still represents what you said accurately.

That can take a lot of pressure off. Instead of thinking about three paragraphs, you're thinking about the sentence in front of you.

It also gives you a chance to listen to the direction. Maybe the opening needs more energy. Maybe a line needs to slow down. Small adjustments are easier when you're not worried about forgetting everything that comes afterward.

## What if reading just doesn't work for you?

Then let's have a conversation.

An interview format can be a good fit for someone who explains things comfortably when another person asks a question. You can speak to an interviewer just off camera, or use a conversational prompt before addressing the lens.

We might ask, "What would you point out first if you were showing this home?" or "What's the question sellers ask you most often before listing?"

Those prompts give you something specific to respond to. We can shape the answer into a focused piece without trying to manufacture a different personality.

Voiceover is another option for certain projects. You don't need to appear in every frame to contribute your perspective.

## Give yourself a little room to settle in

Before recording, read the words aloud. Flag anything that feels awkward. Wear something you can move and sit in comfortably, and ask where you should look.

I also like starting with a question the person knows well rather than treating the first take as the most important one. There is room to adjust.

The goal isn't to erase every pause or make you look like a television presenter. It's to help a potential client meet the capable, approachable professional your existing clients already know.
