"Hi, I'm [name] with [brokerage], and today I'm here at..."

There's nothing wrong with introducing yourself. But before we start a real estate video that way, I want to know whether the viewer has a reason to care yet.

If someone has clicked into a detailed property presentation, an introduction may make perfect sense. If the video appears while they're scrolling, we may be asking for attention before giving them anything interesting to look at or think about.

My preference is to lead with the reason to watch: a distinctive feature, a useful question, or a clear observation about the home. Then we can introduce you where it feels natural.

## The opening should make a promise the video can keep

A good hook tells the viewer what they're about to get.

For a home with a genuine indoor-outdoor connection, that might be: "The best part of this living room is what happens when you open these doors."

For an educational video, it could be: "Before you book your listing photos, check what's visible through the windows."

Those are sample lines, not universal formulas. The right opening depends on the footage and the message. We shouldn't use a dramatic line unless the video delivers something worth the buildup.

I would rather make one specific observation than call every property extraordinary. Specifics give us something to show. Broad praise usually just gives us more adjectives to read.

## Start with the footage, not just the words

Sometimes the most effective opening doesn't need a spoken line at all.

A well-composed view, a doorway opening onto a terrace, or a thoughtful detail can establish the subject before the voiceover begins. The edit can make the viewer curious without immediately explaining everything.

Other videos benefit from an agent appearing right away, especially when the person's explanation is the main value. A useful answer about selling a home should feel like a conversation with someone who can help.

The decision is practical: what does this audience need first?

We can make a visual opening and a spoken opening from the same project, then choose based on where each version will be used. That is different from declaring one format the winner without testing it.

## Where should your name and branding go?

Your name still belongs in the presentation. It just doesn't always have to occupy the first spoken sentence.

Depending on the platform and project, a simple on-screen identification, a natural introduction after the opening, and a clear end card can do the job. Your profile and caption may provide additional context, but a video can also be shared outside that original setting.

That means the piece should still make sense when someone sees it elsewhere. Don't rely entirely on the account name to explain who is speaking or how to contact them.

And required brokerage identification or other advertising disclosures should remain where your brokerage and applicable rules require them. A cleaner opening is not a reason to remove information that needs to be there.

The design goal is clarity, not making the brand disappear.

## Avoid hooks that sound bigger than the property

You don't need to manufacture urgency or suggest the viewer has discovered a secret that everyone else missed.

Try explaining what is genuinely distinctive instead. A flexible layout, a carefully designed kitchen, or a particular relationship to the outdoors may be enough.

The same applies to educational videos. "Here is one thing to check before your shoot" sets a reasonable expectation. "This mistake will ruin your sale" makes a much bigger claim and may not reflect the advice you are actually giving.

Your opening is part of the relationship you're building. It should sound like the professional someone would meet in person.

## Give the ending a job too

Once someone has watched, make the next step clear. A property video might direct them to the full listing or the representing agent. An educational piece might invite them to save the advice or ask a related question.

You don't need five different requests. Choose the action that fits the content.

An end card can carry the contact details without interrupting the main story. Keep it readable and give the viewer enough time to understand it. Also check how the information looks in the actual crop and interface where the video will appear.
