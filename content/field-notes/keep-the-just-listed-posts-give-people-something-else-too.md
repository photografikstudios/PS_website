There's a place for a just-listed post. You're marketing a property, and people should know it's available.

The same goes for a closing or a project you're proud of. You don't need to stop sharing your work.

But when every post is an announcement, someone who isn't buying or selling right now may not find much that's useful to them. And a prospective client may still have no idea how you think or what working with you would feel like.

I would keep the listings and add more of the context around them: the questions you answer, the choices you help people make, and the things you notice that they might miss.

That gives your content somewhere to go between transactions.

## Explain one thing a client finds confusing

You probably answer questions every week that could make useful content.

What should be ready before listing photos? What happens at the first appointment? How should someone think about presenting a room with an unusual layout?

Choose one question and answer it clearly. Don't turn it into a full seminar. Give someone enough information to understand the point, then direct them to a more specific conversation when their situation needs it.

The best topic is often the one you think is too basic. It's familiar to you because you do the work. The person watching may be encountering it for the first time.

You don't need to invent a new opinion every week to be helpful.

## Show a decision, not just a finished result

A beautiful property photo is worth sharing. A brief explanation of a decision behind it can give the viewer more to take away.

You might explain why the outdoor spaces were important to the marketing plan, why you recommended simplifying a room before the shoot, or why a particular feature deserved its own video.

Keep the focus on helping the viewer understand the process. There's no need to make the seller's personal circumstances public or present a client as a lesson in what not to do.

When a real example would reveal private information, use a general explanation or get clear permission before sharing. The content should reflect the care you want future clients to expect from you.

## Give local knowledge a purpose

A neighborhood post can be useful when it offers specific context rather than a generic list of places you like.

Perhaps you can explain a public space, introduce a local business with permission, or show something a visitor might otherwise overlook. Be clear about where you are and keep factual statements accurate.

The connection to your work doesn't have to be forced. An agent who spends time in an area can show genuine interest in the place without turning every conversation into a sales pitch.

Just be careful not to make unsupported claims about safety, residents, school quality, or who belongs in a neighborhood. You can describe observable features and point people toward reliable information without making those judgments for them.

## Let people see how you communicate

A short answer on camera gives a viewer a sample of your approach.

Are you clear? Do you explain without talking down to people? Can you acknowledge that a question depends on the situation rather than forcing a simple answer where one doesn't belong?

Those are useful things for a potential client to learn.

You can also use an interview, a voiceover with relevant footage, or a written explanation beside a photograph. Being visible doesn't require making every post a direct-to-camera performance.

Choose formats that suit the message and that you can actually keep producing.

## What could a manageable content mix look like?

As a planning example, you could alternate a client question, a property story, a process explanation, and a local observation. Add active listing announcements when they are relevant.

That is a starting structure, not a rule about posting frequency or a promised recipe for growth. Some weeks will need more property marketing. Other weeks may offer a useful question worth answering in detail.

What matters is whether the mix helps someone understand your work. A calendar full of unrelated filler doesn't solve the problem just because it is full.

## Make the content useful after the post goes live

A good answer can also help in an email conversation or sit on a relevant page of your website. A project explanation can become part of a portfolio story. Plan those uses and the required media permissions before production.

That's the space worth filling.
